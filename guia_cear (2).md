# CEAR Medical Residency Exam Study Guide: Endocrinology I (Thyroid & Adrenal)

## 1. Hypothyroidism: Classification and Clinical Presentation

### 1.1. Etiological Classification
Hypothyroidism results from a metabolic state of deficient thyroid hormone production. 

| Category | Causes |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Primary** | Autoimmune (Hashimoto’s, Atrophic), Iatrogenic (Post-I131, Post-thyroidectomy, Neck Radiation), Drugs (Iodine excess/amiodarone, Lithium, Interferon $\alpha$), Congenital, Iodine deficiency, Infiltrative (**Hemochromatosis, Scleroderma**, Riedel, Sarcoidosis, Amyloidosis). |
| **Transient** | Silent thyroiditis (including puerperal), Subacute thyroiditis (De Quervain), Treatment suspension, Post-I131/Subtotal thyroidectomy for Graves'. |
| **Secondary (Central)** | TSH deficiency, Hypopituitarism (Tumors, Surgery, Sheehan Syndrome, Trauma), **Bexarotene treatment**. |

> **STRATEGIST'S EDGE:** Globally, iodine deficiency remains the most common cause. However, in iodine-sufficient regions, autoimmune disease (Hashimoto's) is the primary etiology.

### 1.2. Congenital Hypothyroidism and Cretinism
Incidence is 1/4000 live births; 85% are due to thyroid dysgenesis.

**High-Yield Warning Signs (Neonates):**
*   Macroglossia and hoarse cry.
*   Persistent jaundice and constipation.
*   Lethargy, somnolence, and feeding refusal.
*   Open posterior fontanelle and delayed bone age.
*   **Late umbilical cord detachment (> 15 days).**

**Cretinism Pathognomonic Features:**
*   Severe intellectual deficit.
*   **Spasticity and tremor.**
*   Linear growth failure.

**Diagnostics:**
*   **Heel Prick (FEI):** Must be performed at **48–72 hours** of life. Tests before this window may yield false positives due to the physiological TSH surge.
*   **Thresholds:** TSH < 20 mU/L (Normal); **TSH > 40 mU/L** (Primary Congenital Hypothyroidism).

### 1.3. Autoimmune and Clinical Variants
*   **Hashimoto’s Thyroiditis:** Chronic lymphocytic infiltration presenting with a firm, irregular goiter.
*   **Atrophic Thyroiditis:** Final stage of autoimmune destruction with minimal residual tissue.

**CLINICAL PEARL: Functional Distinctions**
*   **Subclinical Hypothyroidism:** Normal T4L with High TSH. (Confirm elevation persists >3 months before treatment).
*   **Clinical Hypothyroidism:** Low T4L with High TSH (>10 mU/L).
*   **Central Hypothyroidism:** Low T4L with inappropriately **Normal or Low TSH**.

## 2. Diagnostic Algorithm for Thyroid Dysfunction

### 2.1. Primary Screening Logic
Management is dictated by the initial TSH determination:

*   **IF TSH is Elevated:** Determine Free T4 (T4L).
    *   **T4L Low:** Primary Hypothyroidism. Check ATPO.
        *   *ATPO+:* Autoimmune Hypothyroidism. Initiate LT4.
        *   *ATPO-:* Investigate other causes (drugs, infiltrative).
    *   **T4L Normal:** Mild (Subclinical) Hypothyroidism.
        *   *Treat if:* TSH > 10, symptomatic, ATPO+, or pregnancy/desire for gestation.
        *   *Monitor if:* Asymptomatic and ATPO-.
*   **IF TSH is Normal:**
    *   **Pituitary Disease Suspected?**
        *   *Yes:* Measure T4L. If low, evaluate for Central Hypothyroidism or Sick Euthyroid Syndrome.
        *   *No:* No further tests needed.

### 2.2. Specific Populations
*   **Pregnancy:** Universal screening is not demonstrated to be beneficial. Evaluate high-risk patients only. Use T4L (not total). 
    *   **Trimester-specific TSH targets:** < 2.5 mU/L (1st); < 3.0 mU/L (2nd/3rd).
*   **Elderly:** Incidence increases with age (average Dx at 60 years). Use a "start low, go slow" dosing approach.

## 3. Therapeutic Management of Hypothyroidism

### 3.1. Standard Substitution Therapy
*   **LT4 Dosing:** Standard replacement is **1.6 µg/Kg/day** (single morning dose).
*   **Cardiovascular Disease/Elderly:** Start with **12.5–25 µg/day**. Increase by 12.5–25 µg increments every 2 months until TSH is normalized.

### 3.2. Monitoring Protocols
1.  **Initial:** Repeat TSH 2 months after dose adjustment.
2.  **Stabilization:** Control at 6 months, then annually.
3.  **Long-term:** Stable patients may be monitored every 3–4 years.

**Dose Adjustment Triggers:**
*   **Pregnancy:** Often requires a **50% increase** in LT4 dose.
*   **Drug Interactions:** Estrogens, oral anticoagulants, and oral hypoglycemics.

## 4. Myxedema Coma: Emergency Management

### 4.1. Definition and Pathogenesis
Extreme hypothyroidism characterized by altered sensorium, hypoventilation (hypercapnia), hyponatremia (dilutional), and bradycardia/diastolic hypertension.

### 4.2. Clinical Triggers and Diagnosis
*   **Triggers:** Infection (35%, mainly respiratory), cold exposure, CNS depressants.
*   **ECG:** Low voltage, prolonged QT, flattened/inverted T waves.
*   **Lab:** Low T3/T4, high CPK (MM fraction), respiratory acidosis.

### 4.3. Therapeutic Protocol
1.  **Supportive:** O2, volume expansion (avoid hypotonic fluids), warming if Temp < 30°C.
2.  **Glucocorticoids:** **MUST BE GIVEN BEFORE T4.** Hydrocortisone 50mg/6h (or 100mg IV bolo then 200mg/24h) to prevent acute adrenal crisis.
3.  **Hormones:** T4 IV (500 µg bolo, then 50–100 µg/day). T3 IV may be added in severe cases (10–25 µg bolo).

## 5. Thyrotoxicosis and Graves' Disease

### 5.1. Etiology and Pathophysiology
*   **Hyperthyroidism:** Synthesis increase (Graves’, Toxic Adenoma).
*   **Thyrotoxicosis without Hyperthyroidism:** Destruction/release (Thyroiditis) or exogenous (Factitious).

### 5.2. Graves' Disease Profile
Autoimmune (TSI antibodies).
*   **Triad:** Diffuse goiter, ophthalmopathy, pretibial mixedema (orange-peel skin).
*   **Atypical Presentation:** "Apathetic hyperthyroidism" in elderly (fatigue, AF, weight loss).

### 5.3. Graves' Ophthalmopathy (NO SPECS)
Progression: **N**(None), **O**(Only lid retraction), **S**(Soft tissue edema), **P**(Proptosis >22mm), **E**(Extraocular muscle/diplopia), **C**(Corneal), **S**(Sight loss).
*   **Exam Warning:** Smoking and Radioiodine (I-131) treatment are major risk factors for progression.

---

## 6. Hyperthyroidism Diagnostic and Treatment Framework

### 6.1. Diagnostic Logic
Start with TSH/T4L. If TSH is suppressed:
1.  **High T4L/T3:** Primary Thyrotoxicosis.
2.  **Scintigraphy (Gammagrafía):**
    *   *High/Diffuse Uptake:* Graves'.
    *   *Focal/Nodular:* Toxic Adenoma or Toxic Multinodular Goiter.
    *   *Low Uptake:* Thyroiditis, Iodine excess, Factitious.

### 6.2. Pharmacological Interventions
| Medication | Half-life | Strategist's Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Methimazole (MMI)** | 6 hours | Preferred agent. Dose: 10–30 mg. |
| **Propylthiouracil (PTU)** | 1-2 hours | **Preferred in 1st Trimester** and **Thyroid Storm** (blocks T4 $\rightarrow$ T3 conversion). |

> **CLINICAL PEARL: Agranulocytosis Monitoring**
> Patients on MMI/PTU must be warned: **Fever or sore throat (pharyngitis)** requires an immediate cessation of the drug and a **mandatory CBC (hemograma)** to rule out agranulocytosis (<1% risk but fatal).

### 6.3. Definitive Treatments
*   **Radioiodine (I-131):** First-line in many regions. Contraindicated in pregnancy.
*   **Surgery:** Indicated for large goiters or I-131 rejection. Requires pre-op euthyroidism.

## 7. Thyroid Emergencies: Thyroid Storm

### 7.1. Triggers and Clinical Features
Extreme hypermetabolism (Hyperthermia, AF, CNS agitation/coma, jaundice). Triggered by surgery, infection, or iodine load.

### 7.2. Management Protocol (The Order Matters)
1.  **Beta-blockers:** Propranolol (blocks adrenergic action and peripheral T4 $\rightarrow$ T3).
2.  **Thionamides:** PTU preferred (High dose loading).
3.  **Iodine (Lugol/SSKI):** **MUST WAIT 1 HOUR AFTER THIONAMIDES.**
    *   **Reason:** To prevent the iodine from being used as substrate for new hormone synthesis (**Wolff-Chaikoff effect** logic: you must block the "factory" with thionamides before providing the iodine).
4.  **Glucocorticoids:** Dexamethasone or Hydrocortisone.

---

## 8. Thyroiditis and Specialized Syndromes

### 8.1. Classification
*   **Subacute (De Quervain):** Post-viral, painful, high ESR, **Low Iodine Uptake**. 
*   **Euthyroid Sick Syndrome:** Adaptive. Low T3 (mild illness); Low T3/T4 (severe illness = poor prognosis).

### 8.2. Amiodarone-Induced Dysfunction
High-yield "Next Step" management question:
*   **Type 1 (Jod-Basedow):** Excess synthesis in underlying goiter. **Color Flow Doppler: Increased vascularity.**
*   **Type 2 (Destructive):** Inflammatory release. **Color Flow Doppler: Reduced vascularity.**

---

## 9. Adrenal Pathology: Cushing’s Syndrome

### 9.1. Classification
*   **ACTH-Dependent:** Cushing's Disease (Pituitary - most common endogenous), Ectopic (Lung).
*   **ACTH-Independent:** Adrenal tumors.
*   **Iatrogenic:** Most common cause overall.

### 9.2. Diagnostic Strategy
*   **Step 1: Confirm Hypercortisolism:** 24h Urine Free Cortisol (3x normal), 1mg Dexamethasone suppression (Cortisol > 5 µg/dL), or Midnight Salivary Cortisol.
*   **Step 2: ACTH Localization:**
    *   *ACTH < 5 pg/mL:* ACTH-Independent $\rightarrow$ Adrenal CT.
    *   *ACTH > 15 pg/mL:* ACTH-Dependent $\rightarrow$ Step 3.
*   **Step 3: Source Identification:** High-dose Dexamethasone (suppresses >50% in Pituitary Disease) or Petrosal Sinus Sampling.

### 9.3. Adrenal Incidentaloma
Mass $\geq$ 1 cm.
*   **Malignancy Criteria (Consider Surgery):**
    *   Size > 4 cm.
    *   **Density > 20 UH.**
    *   **Contrast Washout < 40%.**

## 10. Mineralocorticoid Excess and Adrenal Insufficiency

### 10.1. Primary Hyperaldosteronism (Conn’s)
*   **Clinical:** Refractory HTN, Hypokalemia (50% are normokalemic), "Escape" (no edema).
*   **Screening (Phase 1):** Both must be positive: **ARR > 750 pmol/L** AND **Aldosterone > 450 pmol/L**.
*   **Confirmation (Phase 2):** Saline load test (failure to suppress).
*   **Etiology (Phase 3):** CT scan; if > 40 years, Adrenal Vein Sampling (AVS) is mandatory before surgery to distinguish unilateral adenoma from bilateral hyperplasia.

### 10.2. Adrenal Insufficiency (Addison’s)
*   **Primary:** Destruction of cortex. **21-hydroxylase antibodies** are the marker for autoimmune adrenalitis.
*   **Syndromes:** SPA 1 (Candidiasis, Hypopara, Addison); SPA 2 (Thyroid, T1DM, Addison).

### 10.3. Differential Diagnosis
*   **Liddle's:** Low Renin, Low Aldosterone, HTN. (Amiloride sensitive).
*   **AME:** Cortisol activates MR receptors due to 11$\beta$-HSD2 deficiency. (Dexamethasone sensitive).

# Comprehensive Gynecology Study Guide for Medical Residency Examinations

## 1. Uterine Oncology: Sarcomas and Endometrial Cancer

### 1.1 Malignant Connective Tumors (Uterine Sarcomas)
Uterine sarcomas represent 2–5% of all malignant uterine tumors. They arise from the atypical proliferation of mesenchymal elements (smooth muscle, stroma, or vessels).

| Category (GOG) | Frequency | Mean Age | Key Diagnostic/Prognostic Criteria |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Leiomyosarcomas** | 50–75% | 50 years | **Diagnosis:** Hypercellularity, atypia, and mitotic index. **Prognosis:** 83% 5-year survival in Stage 1-2. Most deaths occur within 4 years. |
| **Endometrial Stromal Sarcomas** | 15–20% | Early | **Threshold:** 10 mitosis per high-power field (HPF) differentiates low-grade from high-grade. Linked to prior pelvic radiation. |
| **Carcinosarcomas (Mixed Müllerian Homologous)** | Aggressive | 60 years | Rapid regional lymphatic and vascular spread to lung and liver. |
| **Mixed Mesodermal Sarcomas (Heterologous)** | Aggressive | 60 years | **Classic Sign:** "Meat-wash" (lavado de carne) discharge. 50% limited to uterus at diagnosis. |

**Exam Pearl:** Leiomyosarcomas are the most frequent uterine sarcomas and can arise from sarcomatous degeneration of a pre-existing myoma.

**Management:** Total extrafascial anexohysterectomy is the gold standard. Radiotherapy improves local control but not overall survival. Doxorubicin is the drug of choice for high mitotic counts (>20 HPF) or deep invasion.

### 1.2 Adenocarcinoma of the Endometrium
Malignant tumor of the mucosal epithelium.

*   **Type 1 (Perimenopausal):** Estrogen-dependent, associated with hyperplasia, well-differentiated, and favorable prognosis.
*   **Type 2 (Elderly):** Estrogen-independent, no relation to hyperplasia, poorly differentiated (serous/clear cell), and poor prognosis.

**Epidemiology and Risk Factors:**
*   **Hyperestrogenism:** Obesity (androgenic type), chronic anovulation, and estrogen-only therapy.
*   **Medication:** Prolonged Tamoxifen use (increased risk due to estrogenic effect on the uterus).
*   **Age:** Peak incidence between 55–65 years (85% are >50 years).

### 1.3 Endometrial Hyperplasia Management
Atypical hyperplasia is the direct precursor to endometrial carcinoma.

| Hyperplasia Type | Therapeutic Conduct |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Simple/Complex without atypia** | Progestogens or combined oral contraceptives. **With desire for fertility:** Ovulation induction is required. |
| **Atypical** | **Surgical (Mandatory):** Hysterectomy or anexohysterectomy. **If fertility desired:** High-dose hormonal therapy with strict histological re-evaluation. |

### 1.4 Staging and Treatment of Endometrial Cancer
Staging is surgical-pathological. **Note:** Up to stage IVA, tumors are divided by histological grade: **G1** (well-differentiated), **G2** (moderately differentiated), and **G3** (indiferentiated).

**Surgical Requirements:** Laparotomy (median infraumbilical), peritoneal cytology, abdominal exploration, anexohysterectomy, and evaluation of miometrial/cervical extension.

**FIGO Staging and Management:**
1.  **Stage IA:** Limited to endometrium or <1/2 miometrial invasion.
    *   *Treatment:* Anexohysterectomy (AHT). If G3, add pelvic/para-aortic lymphadenectomy.
2.  **Stage IB:** >1/2 miometrial invasion.
    *   *Treatment:* AHT + lymphadenectomy + Pelvic Radiotherapy (RT).
3.  **Stage II:** Invasion of cervical stroma.
    *   *Treatment:* Wertheim-Meigs operation.
4.  **Stage III:** Local/regional spread.
    *   **IIIA (Serosa/adnexa):** AHT + Pelvic RT.
    *   **IIIB (Vagina/parametria):** AHT (if possible) + RT + Brachytherapy (BT).
    *   **IIIC (Pelvic/aortic nodes):** RT + BT.
5.  **Stage IV:** Bladder/rectal mucosa (IVA) or distant metastasis (IVB).
    *   *Treatment:* RT + BT. Hormonal therapy (Medroxyprogesterone) for advanced cases with positive receptors.

## 2. Ovarian Pathology: Benign, Pseudotumoral, and Screening

### 2.1 Diagnostic Workup of Adnexal Masses
*   **Primary Approach:** Physical exam, Transvaginal Ultrasound (TVUS) with Doppler, and Tumor Markers.
*   **Gold Standard:** Histological confirmation via surgical approach (laparotomy/laparoscopy).

### 2.2 Differential Diagnosis of Cysts
| Feature | Simple Cyst (Presumed Benign) | Complex Cyst (Presumed Malignant) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Content** | Liquid | Mixed or solid |
| **Capsule** | Fine/Thin | Thick |
| **Septa** | None | Fine or thick |
| **Papillae** | None | Present |
| **Doppler** | Negative | Positive (High vascularity) |

### 2.3 Diagnostic Algorithms by Age
**Patients < 45 Years Old:**
*   **Benign Findings:** 
    *   If **< 8 cm**: Observation (Control in 3–6 months + OCPs).
    *   If **>= 8 cm** or persistence: Laparoscopy (initially conservative: cystectomy).
*   **Malignant/Complex:** Exploratory laparotomy for diagnosis, staging, and treatment.

**Patients > 45 Years Old:**
*   **Benign Findings:**
    *   If **< 5 cm**: Observation (Control in 3–6 months + OCPs).
    *   If **>= 5 cm** or persistence: Laparoscopy (Bilateral Oophorectomy).
*   **Malignant/Complex:** Exploratory laparotomy for diagnosis, staging, and treatment.

### 2.4 Tumor Markers and Pseudotumoral Lesions
**Tumor Markers (Strict Source List):**
*   **AFP (Alpha-fetoprotein):** Germ cell tumors (Yolk sac).
*   **ACE (Carcinoembryonic Antigen):** Colon/GI origin.
*   **Beta-HCG:** Trophoblastic disease and Germ cell tumors (Coriocarcinoma).
*   **HPL (Placental Lactogen):** Implantation site tumors.
*   **Inhibin:** Granulosa cell tumors.
*   **CA 125:** Epithelial ovarian tumors.
*   **CA 19.9:** Mucinous tumors.
*   **CA 15.3:** Linked to breast cancer.

**Pseudotumoral Processes:**
*   **Solid:** Luteoma of pregnancy (can produce virilization), Stromal hyperplasia, Massive edema (related to torsion).
*   **Cystic:** Follicular cysts (most common, avg 2cm), Corpus luteum cysts, Theca lutein cysts (bilateral, stimulus from HCG), Endometriomas.

## 3. Ovarian Cancer: Epithelial and Non-Epithelial

### 3.1 Epithelial Ovarian Cancer (EOC)
The most common (85–90%) and lethal gynecological cancer.
*   **Serous:** Most frequent (50% of EOC). Often bilateral (50%).
*   **Mucinous:** 2nd most frequent. Large size (up to 50cm). Linked to pseudomyxoma peritonei.
*   **Endometrioid:** 30–50% bilateral; variant of low malignancy.
*   **Brenner:** Rare, usually benign.

### 3.2 Epidemiology and Heredity
*   **Protective Factors:** Pregnancy (2 pregnancies + 5 years of OCPs reduces risk by 70%), breastfeeding.
*   **Hereditary Patterns:**
    1.  **Site-specific:** Ovarian only, autosomal dominant.
    2.  **Breast/Ovarian (BRCA):** BRCA1 (28-44% risk), BRCA2 (27% risk).
    3.  **Lynch II:** Colon, breast, and endometrial cancer association.

### 3.3 Staging and Surgical Principles
*   **Optimal Cytoreduction:** The most important prognostic factor. Aim is to leave **no residual lesion > 2 cm**.
*   **FIGO Stages:** I (Ovaries only), II (Pelvic extension), III (Extrapelvic peritoneal metastasis/nodes), IV (Distant/Extraperitoneal).

### 3.4 Non-Epithelial Tumors (Germ Cell and Sex Cord)
*   **Germ Cell (Ages 13–30):**
    *   **Dysgerminoma:** Most common malignant germ cell tumor. Radiosensitive.
    *   **Yolk Sac (Endodermal Sinus):** Produces AFP; aggressive growth.
*   **Sex Cord Tumors:**
    *   **Granulosa Cell:** Low malignancy, produces estrogen (feminization, endometrial hyperplasia), marker: Inhibin.
    *   **Arrenoblastoma:** Produces virilization/defeminization.

### 3.5 Ovarian Cancer and Pregnancy
Most are diagnosed in the 1st or 2nd trimester. 35% are borderline and only 4-5% are malignant. **Crucial:** 25% of cases are complicated by **torsion**.

## 4. Breast Pathology: Diagnosis and Benign Disease

### 4.1 Imaging and BI-RADS Classification
| BI-RADS | Description | Action |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **0** | Incomplete | Additional imaging required |
| **1 / 2** | Negative / Benign | Routine control |
| **3** | Probably Benign | 6-month follow-up |
| **4** | Suspicious | Biopsy (Subdivided A, B, C) |
| **5** | Suggestive of Malignancy | Biopsy (> 95% malignancy) |
| **6** | Known Malignancy | Biopsy-proven |

**Malignancy Characteristics:** Spiculated margins, clustered microcalcifications, heterogeneous density, skin retraction.

### 4.2 Benign Conditions and Risk Categories
**Breast Cancer Risk (Pathological Classification):**
*   **No Risk:** Duct ectasia, simple adenosis, apocrine changes.
*   **Low Increase Risk:** Proliferation without atypia (Sclerosing adenosis, ductal hyperplasia).
*   **Medium Risk:** Proliferation with atypia (Atypical ductal/lobular hyperplasia).
*   **High Risk:** Atypical ductal hyperplasia with family history, Lobular Carcinoma in situ (LCIS), Ductal Carcinoma in situ (DCIS).

**Specific Benign Entities:**
*   **Fibroadenoma:** Most frequent benign tumor; ages 15–30; mobile, "hard-elastic."
*   **Phyllodes Tumor:** Sudden growth spurt; risk of sarcomatous degeneration.
*   **Intraductal Papilloma:** Main cause of bloody (sanguinolent) nipple discharge.

## 5. Breast Cancer: Classification, Staging, and Management

### 5.1 Molecular Subtypes
1.  **Luminal A:** Most common (40%). ER+/PR+, HER2-, low proliferation.
2.  **Luminal B:** ER+, HER2 variable, high proliferation. Worse prognosis than A.
3.  **HER2-positive:** (15%) High HER2 expression, aggressive.
4.  **Basal-like (Triple Negative):** ER-, PR-, HER2-. Linked to BRCA1 mutation.

### 5.2 TNM Staging (7th ed)
*   **T (Tumor):**
    *   **T1mi:** <= 0.1 cm.
    *   **T1a:** > 0.1 to 0.5 cm.
    *   **T1b:** > 0.5 to 1 cm.
    *   **T1c:** > 1 to 2 cm.
    *   **T2:** > 2 to 5 cm; **T3:** > 5 cm; **T4:** Wall/skin invasion.
*   **N (Nodes):**
    *   **pN1mi:** Micrometastasis (0.2–2 mm).
    *   **pN1a:** 1–3 axillary nodes.
    *   **N2a:** Fixed axillary nodes. **N3a:** Infraclavicular. **N3c:** Supraclavicular.

### 5.3 Treatment Protocols
**TALA (Wide Tumorectomy with Axillary Lymphadenectomy):** The standard technique to obtain maximum info with minimum mutilation in Staging I and II.

**Conservative Surgery Contraindications:**
*   **Absolute:** First half of pregnancy, multicentricity, prior RT to breast, difficult follow-up.
*   **Relative:** Large tumor-to-breast volume ratio, collagen diseases (for RT).

**Systemic Control (Hormonal Therapy):**
*   **Competitive:** Tamoxifen.
*   **Ablative:** LHRH analogs.
*   **Additve:** Progesterone; **Substitutive:** Estrogens.

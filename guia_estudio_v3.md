# Medical Residency Examination Comprehensive Study Guide

## 1. Gastroenterology: Chronic Diarrhea and Functional Disorders

This section synthesizes diagnostic classifications and clinical frameworks for chronic diarrhea and Irritable Bowel Syndrome (IBS), optimized for rapid clinical correlation and examination performance.

**Clinical Definitions and Quantitative Thresholds**
*   **Diarrhea (Sign):** Quantitative increase in stool water or weight exceeding **200–225 ml/g in 24 hours**.
*   **Chronic Diarrhea:** Symptoms persisting for more than **4 weeks**.

**"Red Flag" (Signos de Alarma) Indicators**
The presence of any of the following requires immediate investigation to exclude organic pathology:
*   **Duration:** Clinical course of fewer than three months.
*   **Nocturnal Symptoms:** Diarrhea or pain that **awakens the patient from sleep**.
*   **Hematology/Systemic:** **Anemia**, fever, or documented **weight loss**.
*   **Bleeding:** Visible or occult digestive bleeding.
*   **Stool Characteristics:** Stools that are bloody, greasy (steatorrhea), or of exceptionally large volume.
*   **Physical Exam:** Positive findings such as abdominal masses or localized tenderness.

**Mechanistic Classification of Chronic Diarrhea**

| Mechanism | Stool Characteristics | Response to Fasting | Fecal Osmotic Gap |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Secretory** | Voluminous, aqueous, and generally painless. | **Persistent**; does not improve with fasting. | **Normal (< 25 mOsm/kg)** |
| **Osmotic** | Secondary to unabsorbed nutrients/laxatives. | **Disappears** with fasting or cessation of offending agent. | **Elevated (> 50 mOsm/kg)** |
| **Malabsorptive** | Pale, pasty, or aqueous; adherent and foul-smelling. | May decrease, but underlying defect persists. | Variable; associated with steatorrhea. |
| **Inflammatory** | Accompanied by pain, fever, and hemorrhage. | Does not resolve; associated with protein exudation. | Variable; presence of leukocytes/calprotectin. |

**The Fecal Osmotic Gap Formula**
To differentiate between secretory and osmotic mechanisms, use the following calculation:
*   **Calculation:** $2 \times (\text{Stool } Na^+ + \text{Stool } K^+) \le \text{Stool Osmolarity}$
*   **Reference:** Normal stool osmolarity is approximately **300 mOsm/kg**.

**IBS Diagnostic Framework: Rome IV Criteria**
To diagnose Irritable Bowel Syndrome, the patient must report recurrent abdominal pain averaging **at least 1 day per week during the last 3 months**, associated with two or more of the following:
*   [ ] Related to defecation.
*   [ ] Associated with a change in the frequency of stool.
*   [ ] Associated with a change in the form (appearance) of stool.

*Strategic Timing Requirement: Criteria must be met for the last 3 months with symptom onset at least 6 months prior to diagnosis.*

**Differential Diagnosis Checklist (Exclusion of IBS)**
The following clinical features suggest organic disease and exclude a functional IBS diagnosis:
*   **Demographics:** Onset in senescence (advanced age).
*   **Progression:** A progressive rather than episodic clinical course.
*   **Fasting Response:** Diarrhea persisting after 48 hours of fasting (indicates a **Secretory** mechanism).
*   **Nocturnal Symptoms:** Diarrhea occurring during sleep.
*   **Systemic Indices:** Anemia, elevated **ESR**, fever, or involuntary weight loss.
*   **Laboratory findings:** Stool volume **> 200–300 ml/day**; presence of leukocytes or blood in stool.
*   **Steatorrhea:** Qualitative or quantitative evidence of fat malabsorption.

## 2. Gastroenterology: Malabsorption Syndromes and Specific Enteropathies

**Physiopathological Mapping: Bile Acid Circulation**
Failures in the enterohepatic circulation define specific diagnostic pathways:
*   **Synthesis Failure:** Caused by hepatic dysfunction (e.g., *Cirrhosis*).
*   **Secretion Failure:** Caused by canalicular dysfunction (e.g., *Primary Biliary Cholangitis*).
*   **Maintenance Failure:** Bacterial deconjugation due to proliferation/stasis (e.g., *Jejunal diverticulosis*).
*   **Reabsorption Failure:** Dysfunction of the distal ileum (e.g., *Crohn's Disease*).

**Diagnostic Testing Suite**
*   **D-Xylose Test:** Evaluates proximal small intestine mucosal integrity. Following a 25g dose, urinary excretion of **< 4.5g over 5 hours** indicates duodenal or jejunal disease.
*   **Hydrogen Breath Test:** Non-invasive gold standard for identifying:
    *   **Bacterial Overgrowth (SIBO):** Utilizing glucose as the substrate.
    *   **Lactose Intolerance:** Utilizing lactose to detect malabsorption/fermentation.
*   **Schilling Test:** Critical for localizing the cause of B12 deficiency.

**High-Yield Disease Profiles**

*   **Whipple’s Disease**
    *   **Etiology:** *Tropheryma whippeli* (Gram-positive actinobacteria).
    *   **Biopsy Findings:** **PAS+ Macrophages** in the lamina propria.
    *   **Strategic Diagnostic Key:** The presence of bacilli **outside the macrophages** is the primary indicator of **active disease or relapse**.
    *   **Treatment:** TMS-SMZ (Trimethoprim-Sulfamethoxazole) for **1 year**.

*   **Tropical Sprue**
    *   **Etiology:** Postulated infection by *Klebsiella pneumoniae*, *Enterobacter cloacae*, or *Escherichia coli*.
    *   **Clinical Pearl:** Unlike Celiac disease, it affects the **entire intestine** with equal intensity and **does not improve** with a gluten-free diet.
    *   **Treatment:** Tetracyclines for up to **6 months** plus Folic Acid.

*   **Celiac Disease**
    *   **Etiology:** Permanent intolerance to gluten/gliadin (wheat, barley, rye).
    *   **Biopsy Findings:** "Flat mucosa," villous atrophy, crypt hypertrophy, and mononuclear infiltrate.
    *   **Treatment:** Strict, lifelong **gluten-free diet**.

*   **Steatorrhea Quantification (Differential Guide)**
    *   **Definition:** Excretion of fecal fat **> 7g/day**.
    *   **Small Intestine Disease:** Typically ranges between **15–25g/day**.
    *   **Pancreatic Insufficiency:** Characteristically severe, often exceeding **32g/day**.

# Medical Flashcards

## Card 1

**Q:** In breast imaging, how do the contours of a malignant lesion typically differ from those of a benign lesion?

**A:** Malignant lesions have irregular, diffuse, or spiculated contours, while benign ones are regular and net.

---

## Card 2

**Q:** According to the BI-RADS classification, what is the required management for a category 0 (Incomplete) result?

**A:** Completion of the evaluation with additional imaging studies.

---

## Card 3

**Q:** What is the estimated malignancy risk percentage for a breast lesion classified as BI-RADS 3?

**A:** The risk is between 0.5% and 2%.

---

## Card 4

**Q:** Which BI-RADS category indicates a 'suspicious abnormality' and necessitates a biopsy?

**A:** BI-RADS 4.

---

## Card 5

**Q:** What specific microcalcification patterns are characteristic of a BI-RADS 5 (highly suggestive of malignancy) lesion?

**A:** Irregular microcalcifications with a linear, ductal, or arboriform arrangement.

---

## Card 6

**Q:** In the case of a suspicious cytology result from a fine-needle aspiration (PAAF) but no visible imaging finding, which study should be performed next?

**A:** Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI).

---

## Card 7

**Q:** What is the most common form of hyperthyroidism encountered during pregnancy?

**A:** Graves' Disease (toxic diffuse goiter).

---

## Card 8

**Q:** At what age does the onset of a new headache become a clinical 'red flag' for serious underlying disorders?

**A:** After the age of 55.

---

## Card 9

**Q:** Which physical movement often exacerbates the headache pain in patients with meningitis?

**A:** Eye movements.

---

## Card 10

**Q:** How does the headache associated with a brain tumor typically change with physical activity or posture?

**A:** It worsens with exercise or changes in body position.

---

## Card 11

**Q:** A MIDAS (Migraine Disability Assessment Score) of 0 to 5 points corresponds to which disability grade?

**A:** Grade I (minimal or infrequent disability).

---

## Card 12

**Q:** What is the classification time limit for diagnosing 'New Daily Persistent Headache' (NDPH)?

**A:** The headache must have an evolution of up to 3 days.

---

## Card 13

**Q:** What is the primary clinical difference between the pain of Trigeminal Neuralgia and a Cluster headache?

**A:** Trigeminal Neuralgia pain is transfictive and superficial, whereas Cluster pain is deep and constant.

---

## Card 14

**Q:** Which physical exam finding is essentially absent in a diagnosis of primary Trigeminal Neuralgia?

**A:** There is no objective loss of sensation.

---

## Card 15

**Q:** What is the most frequent cause of $T_{3}$ toxicosis?

**A:** Toxic Adenoma.

---

## Card 16

**Q:** What phenomenon describes hyperthyroidism induced by an excess of iodine intake?

**A:** Jod-Basedow phenomenon.

---

## Card 17

**Q:** What is the characteristic iodine uptake pattern in a scintigram for Graves' Disease?

**A:** Increased uptake with a diffuse, homogeneous distribution.

---

## Card 18

**Q:** What is the treatment of choice for a Toxic Adenoma?

**A:** Radioactive iodine ($I^{131}$) or surgery.

---

## Card 19

**Q:** In the context of Subacute Thyroiditis, what occurs to the thyroid's iodine uptake during the thyrotoxic phase?

**A:** There is low or suppressed iodine uptake.

---

## Card 20

**Q:** How does the dermopathy associated with Graves' Disease typically present physically?

**A:** As hard, non-pitting swelling in the pretibial region and dorsum of the feet with an 'orange peel' appearance.

---

## Card 21

**Q:** Which medication is noted as a specific pathogenic factor that can trigger a Thyroid Storm?

**A:** Amiodarone.

---

## Card 22

**Q:** What is the estimated mortality rate for a patient in a Thyroid Crisis/Storm even with appropriate treatment?

**A:** Approximately 30%.

---

## Card 23

**Q:** What is the recommended total duration of initial Diabetes Education for Self-Management (EDAM) for persons with DM2?

**A:** Between 10 and 12 hours total.

---

## Card 24

**Q:** When is the first reinforcement (booster) of EDAM suggested for patients with DM2?

**A:** One year after completing the initial education program.

---

## Card 25

**Q:** How is 'chronic diarrhea' defined in terms of duration?

**A:** Symptoms lasting for more than 4 weeks.

---

## Card 26

**Q:** What formula is used to estimate fecal osmolarity?

**A:** $2 \times ([Na^{+}]_{fecal} + [K^{+}]_{fecal})$.

---

## Card 27

**Q:** Which alarm sign in chronic diarrhea relates to the patient's sleep pattern?

**A:** Diarrhea or pain that awakens the patient from sleep.

---

## Card 28

**Q:** What happens to the stool output in Osmotic Diarrhea when the patient fasts?

**A:** The diarrhea disappears with fasting.

---

## Card 29

**Q:** What is the typical fecal osmotic gap value in cases of Secretory Diarrhea?

**A:** Less than 25.

---

## Card 30

**Q:** What is the most common cause of chronic secretory diarrhea?

**A:** Medications, specifically laxatives.

---

## Card 31

**Q:** What daily stool volume is characteristically exceeded in patients with a VIPoma ('Pancreatic Cholera')?

**A:** Stool volume exceeds 3 liters per day.

---

## Card 32

**Q:** A low fecal pH (< 6) is clinically suggestive of which underlying mechanism?

**A:** Carbohydrate malabsorption.

---

## Card 33

**Q:** In patients over 40 with inflammatory-type chronic diarrhea, what condition must be ruled out as a priority?

**A:** Colorectal cancer.

---

## Card 34

**Q:** What laboratory findings are considered 'against' a diagnosis of Irritable Bowel Syndrome (IBS)?

**A:** Anemia, increased ESR, blood in stool, or stool volume exceeding 200-300 ml/day.

---

## Card 35

**Q:** Which dietary intervention is recommended for IBS patients suffering primarily from gas and bloating?

**A:** A low FODMAP diet.

---

## Card 36

**Q:** What is the quantitative definition of steatorrhea in grams of fecal fat per day?

**A:** Greater than 7 grams per day.

---

## Card 37

**Q:** Which diagnosis must be excluded in a diabetic patient presenting with liquid diarrhea?

**A:** Celiac Disease.

---

## Card 38

**Q:** What is the initial serological test of choice for diagnosing Celiac Disease?

**A:** Anti-tissue transglutaminase (tTG) antibodies.

---

## Card 39

**Q:** What constitutes the 'U' (Uremia) criterion in the CURB-65 score for community-acquired pneumonia?

**A:** Uremia greater than 42 mg/dl.

---

## Card 40

**Q:** What is the recommended antibiotic treatment for a healthy outpatient with NAC and no recent antibiotic use?

**A:** Macrolides (Clarithromycin or Azithromycin) or Doxycycline.

---

## Card 41

**Q:** Which antibiotic class is the preferred single-agent (monotherapy) empiric treatment for hospital-acquired pneumonia without risk of resistant Gram-negative bacilli?

**A:** Piperacillin-tazobactam, Cefepime, or Levofloxacin.

---

## Card 42

**Q:** At what CD4 cell count threshold does the risk for Pneumocystis jirovecii pneumonia significantly increase?

**A:** Below 200 cells/$\mu$L.

---

## Card 43

**Q:** What is the classic radiographic presentation of Pneumocystis jirovecii pneumonia?

**A:** Bilateral interstitial pattern or hilifugal 'butterfly wing' opacities.

---

## Card 44

**Q:** What is the first-line treatment for Pneumocystis jirovecii pneumonia?

**A:** Trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole (TMP-SMX) at 15-20 mg/kg/day for 21 days.

---

## Card 45

**Q:** Which staining method is used to diagnose Cryptosporidium in HIV patients from stool samples?

**A:** Kinyoun stain.

---

## Card 46

**Q:** What colon diameter measurement defines megacolon in the sigmoidal or descending colon?

**A:** A diameter greater than 6.5 cm.

---

## Card 47

**Q:** Which vaccine is specifically indicated for HIV patients with a CD4 count greater than 200?

**A:** Pneumococcal vaccine.

---

## Card 48

**Q:** What non-specific laboratory enzyme is frequently elevated in patients with Pneumocystis jirovecii pneumonia?

**A:** Lactate Dehydrogenase (LDH).

---

## Card 49

**Q:** What are the common non-gastrointestinal symptoms of Microsporidiosis in HIV patients?

**A:** Conjunctivitis and hepatitis.

---

## Card 50

**Q:** What are the primary symptoms that define a 'Thyroid Crisis' or Storm?

**A:** Hyperthermia, profuse sweating, tachycardia, and neurological alterations (delirium, coma).

---

## Card 51

**Q:** In the management of community-acquired pneumonia (NAC), when should empiema be suspected on a radiograph?

**A:** When there is a pleural effusion greater than 1 cm in the lateral decubitus position.

---

## Card 52

**Q:** According to the source, what is the most important risk factor for infection with antibiotic-resistant pneumococci?

**A:** The use of a specific antibiotic in the previous three months.

---

## Card 53

**Q:** What is the characteristic clinical presentation of 'Thunderclap Headache'?

**A:** Intense, explosive headache without a known inciting agent.

---

## Card 54

**Q:** Which conditions must be differentiated from Primary Thunderclap Headache?

**A:** Subarachnoid hemorrhage (HSA), central venous thrombosis, or cervicocephalic dissection.

---

## Card 55

**Q:** What is the clinical significance of a BI-RADS 4A sub-classification?

**A:** It represents a low suspicion of malignancy, ranging from 3% to 49%.

---

## Card 56

**Q:** How does the location of benign breast lesions usually differ from malignant ones?

**A:** Benign lesions are often bilateral, whereas malignant lesions are typically unilateral.

---

## Card 57

**Q:** What are the three main autonomic symptoms associated with SUNCT/SUNA headaches?

**A:** Epiphora (tearing), conjunctival hyperemia (red eye), and nasal congestion.

---

## Card 58

**Q:** What is the diagnostic value of a BI-RADS 6 classification?

**A:** It is reserved for images where malignancy has already been demonstrated by a previous biopsy.

---

## Card 59

**Q:** What are the two components used in the current FIGO/WHO staging for trophoblastic neoplasms?

**A:** Roman numerals for the FIGO stage and Arabic numerals for the WHO prognostic score.

---

## Card 60

**Q:** In Graves' Disease, what is the specific target of the autoantibodies?

**A:** The TSH receptor (anti-TSH receptor antibodies).

---

## Card 61

**Q:** What is the recommended iodine supplementation for hyperthyroid patients in the form of potassium iodide?

**A:** 1 gram of potassium iodide.

---

## Card 62

**Q:** Which condition is the primary differential for Trigeminal Neuralgia if the pain increases specifically while eating or chewing?

**A:** Temporomandibular joint (TMJ) dysfunction.

---

## Card 63

**Q:** What defines 'Chronic Migraine' in terms of headache frequency?

**A:** Headaches occurring $\ge 15$ days per month for at least one month.

---

## Card 64

**Q:** Which symptom triad is highly suggestive of a 'Secretory' type of chronic diarrhea?

**A:** Voluminous, watery, and generally painless stools.

---

## Card 65

**Q:** What is the diagnostic criteria for 'Thyroid Acropachy' in Graves' patients?

**A:** The presence of drumstick-shaped (clubbed) fingers, seen in <1% of patients.

---

## Card 66

**Q:** How should the dermopathy of Graves' Disease be treated according to the source?

**A:** With occlusive bandages and corticosteroids.

---

## Card 67

**Q:** What are the three categories of the 'Severity Spectrum' for Irritable Bowel Syndrome?

**A:** Mild, Moderate, and Grave (Severe).

---

## Card 68

**Q:** Which specific antibiotic is used to treat gas and bloating in IBS as an alternative to diet?

**A:** Rifaximin.

---

## Card 69

**Q:** In the study of chronic diarrhea, what does a fecal osmotic gap greater than 50 indicate?

**A:** An osmotic diarrhea mechanism.

---

## Card 70

**Q:** What is the primary cause of chronic secretory diarrhea involving the terminal ileum?

**A:** Crohn's ileitis or surgical ablation of the terminal ileum (bile acid malabsorption).

---

## Card 71

**Q:** What is the primary role of breast ultrasound in the evaluation of mammary lesions?

**A:** It complements clinical and mammographic study by differentiating solid from cystic lesions.

---

## Card 72

**Q:** What are the common clinical signs of hyperthyroidism observed in pregnancy besides nervousness?

**A:** Insomnia, palpitations (HR > 100 at rest), and excessive sweating.

---

## Card 73

**Q:** Which BI-RADS 4 sub-category carries a 'moderate' suspicion of malignancy (90-94%)?

**A:** BI-RADS 4C.

---

## Card 74

**Q:** How does the treatment for Toxic Multinodular Goiter with Antithyroid drugs affect the physical goiter?

**A:** It normalizes function but stimulates the growth and increases the size of the goiter.

---

## Card 75

**Q:** What is the recommended site of treatment for a patient with NAC and a CURB-65 score indicating 'Moderate Pneumonia'?

**A:** Hospitalization in a general ward.

# Gastroenterology and Malabsorption Study Guide

This study guide provides a comprehensive overview of clinical gastroenterology, focusing on chronic diarrhea, irritable bowel syndrome, and malabsorption syndromes. It synthesizes diagnostic criteria, pathophysiological mechanisms, and clinical management strategies.

## I. Chronic Diarrhea: Definition and Classification

### Definitions
*   **Diarreha (as a symptom):** The expulsion of unformed or abnormally liquid stools with increased frequency.
*   **Diarrhea (as a sign):** A quantitative increase in stool water or weight exceeding 200 to 225 ml/g in 24 hours.
*   **Chronic Diarrhea:** Symptoms persisting for more than four weeks. Unlike acute diarrhea, the majority of chronic cases are non-infectious.

### Clinical Alarm Signs
The presence of these signs necessitates urgent investigation:
*   Duration of less than three months.
*   Nocturnal diarrhea or pain that awakens the patient.
*   Anemia or visible/occult digestive bleeding.
*   Unexplained weight loss or fever.
*   Greasy, bloody, or high-volume stools.
*   Positive findings during physical examination.

### Pathophysiological Classification of Diarrhea

| Mechanism | Characteristics | Key Features |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Secretory** | Voluminous, aqueous, and generally painless. | Persists during fasting; normal fecal osmotic gap (<25). |
| **Osmotic** | Secondary to poor absorption of nutrients. | Disappears with fasting or cessation of the offending substance; High osmotic gap (>50). |
| **Malabsorptive** | Pale, pasty, or aqueous stools; foul-smelling and adherent. | Quantified as steatorrhea (fecal fat >7g/day). |
| **Inflammatory** | Accompanied by pain, fever, and hemorrhage. | Presence of leukocytes or calprotectin in stools; risk of cancer in older patients. |
| **Dysmotility** | Features of secretory type with possible mild steatorrhea. | Linked to conditions like IBS, hyperthyroidism, or diabetes. |
| **Factitious** | Simulated or self-induced (e.g., laxative abuse). | Often seen in women; associated with hypotension and hypokalemia. |

## II. Irritable Bowel Syndrome (IBS)

IBS is a digestive disorder characterized by altered bowel habits and abdominal pain in the absence of detectable structural abnormalities.

### Rome IV Diagnostic Criteria
Recurrent abdominal pain, averaging at least one day per week in the last three months, associated with two or more of the following:
1.  Related to defecation.
2.  Associated with a change in the frequency of stool.
3.  Associated with a change in the form (appearance) of stool.
*Note: Symptoms must have started at least six months prior to diagnosis.*

### Epidemiology and Risk Factors
*   Affects 10–20% of adults, with a higher prevalence in women.
*   **Post-Infectious IBS:** Can be induced by infections like *Campylobacter*, *Salmonella*, or *Shigella*.
*   **Risk Factors:** Longer duration of initial illness, female gender, depression, and significant life stress.
*   **Protective Factors:** Age over 60.

### Treatment Strategies
*   **Dietary:** Low FODMAP diet; avoidance of fructose and artificial sweeteners (sorbitol, mannitol).
*   **Pharmacological:**
    *   *Diarrhea:* Loperamide, Cholestyramine.
    *   *Constipation:* Fiber (Psyllium), Osmotic laxatives (PEG, Magnesium hydroxide), Linaclotide.
    *   *Pain:* Smooth muscle relaxants, Tricyclic antidepressants, SSRIs.
    *   *Gas/Bloating:* Probiotics, Rifaximin.

## III. Malabsorption Syndromes

Malabsorption involves defects in the digestion and absorption of nutrients. It generally presents with steatorrhea, except in cases like primary lactase deficiency.

### Clinical Signs and Nutrient Deficiencies

| Sign/Symptom | Associated Deficiency |
| :--- | :--- |
| Glossitis, cheilosis, stomatitis | Iron, Vitamin B12, Folic Acid |
| Night blindness, Xerophthalmia | Vitamin A |
| Hemorrhagic phenomena, Purpura | Vitamin K |
| Bone pain, Tetany, Paresthesia | Calcium, Vitamin D, Magnesium |
| Peripheral neuropathy | Vitamin B12, Thiamine |
| Edema | Protein (Hypoalbuminemia) |
| Hyperkeratosis, Dermatitis | Vitamin A, Zinc, Essential fatty acids |

### Diagnostic Testing for Malabsorption
*   **Sudan III Staining:** A qualitative screening test for fecal fat.
*   **D-Xylose Test:** Evaluates proximal small intestine mucosa function. Excretion <4.5g in 5 hours suggests duodenal or jejunal disease.
*   **Schilling Test:** Used to determine the cause of Vitamin B12 (Cobalamin) deficiency (e.g., Pernicious anemia vs. Pancreatic insufficiency vs. Bacterial overgrowth).
*   **Secretin Test:** The only test that directly evaluates pancreatic exocrine function through duodenal intubation.

## IV. Specific Pathological Entities

### Whipple’s Disease
*   **Etiology:** *Tropheryma whipplei* (Gram-positive bacillus).
*   **Population:** Predominantly Caucasian males.
*   **Diagnosis:** Biopsy showing PAS-positive macrophages containing bacilli. The presence of bacilli outside macrophages indicates active disease or relapse.
*   **Treatment:** TMS-SMZ for one year.

### Tropical Sprue
*   **Etiology:** Postulated infection (*Klebsiella pneumoniae*, *E. coli*).
*   **Geography:** Common in the Caribbean and Southern India.
*   **Distinction:** Unlike Celiac disease, it affects the entire small intestine and does not respond to a gluten-free diet.
*   **Treatment:** Tetracyclines and Folic Acid for up to six months.

### Celiac Disease
*   **Definition:** Intolerance to gluten (found in wheat, barley, and rye) leading to structural damage of the small intestine.
*   **Diagnosis:** Established via clinical, histological, and immunological response to a gluten-free diet. Biopsies typically show "flat mucosa" (short or absent villi and crypt hypertrophy).

## V. Short-Answer Practice Questions

1.  **How is chronic diarrhea defined in terms of duration?**
    *   It is defined as diarrhea lasting more than four weeks.
2.  **What are the two most common causes of osmotic diarrhea?**
    *   Lactase deficiency (HC malabsorption) and the ingestion of Magnesium-based laxatives.
3.  **What does a fecal osmotic gap of less than 25 suggest?**
    *   It suggests a secretory diarrhea mechanism.
4.  **Identify the triad of "Pancreatic Cholera" (Vipoma).**
    *   Massive watery diarrhea (exceeding 3L/day), hypokalemia, and achlorhydria.
5.  **What histological finding is pathognomonic for Whipple’s Disease?**
    *   The presence of PAS-positive macrophages in the lamina propria.
6.  **Which test is used to differentiate between mucosal disease and pancreatic insufficiency in cases of steatorrhea?**
    *   The D-Xylose test (normal in pancreatic disease, abnormal in mucosal disease) or the Schilling test.
7.  **What is the primary treatment for IBS-related bloating and gas?**
    *   A low FODMAP diet, probiotics, or Rifaximin.

## VII. Glossary of Important Terms

*   **Achlorhydria:** Absence of hydrochloric acid in the gastric secretions.
*   **Anasarca:** Severe generalized edema, often seen in inflammatory diarrhea with protein loss.
*   **Dermatitis Herpetiformis:** A skin manifestation associated with Celiac Disease.
*   **FODMAP:** Fermentable Oligosaccharides, Disaccharides, Monosaccharides, and Polyols; short-chain carbohydrates that can trigger IBS symptoms.
*   **Hiperalgesia Visceral:** Increased sensitivity to pain in the internal organs, a common factor in IBS.
*   **PAS (Periodic Acid-Schiff):** A staining method used to detect polysaccharides, critical for identifying *Tropheryma whipplei*.
*   **SBCID (Small Bowel Chronic Intestinal Dysbiosis/Overgrowth):** Excessive bacterial growth in the small intestine leading to malabsorption.
*   **Steatorrhea:** The presence of excess fat in the stool (greater than 7g/day), indicating fat malabsorption.
*   **Tenesmus:** A continual or recurrent inclination to evacuate the bowels.

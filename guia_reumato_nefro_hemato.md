# Medical Residency Entrance Exam Study Guide: Rheumatology Module

## 1. Rheumatoid Arthritis (RA): Fundamentals and Pathogenesis

**Definition & Epidemiology**
Rheumatoid Arthritis (RA) is a chronic, systemic inflammatory autoimmune disease of unknown etiology, characterized by symmetric peripheral polyarthritis. It is the most common form of chronic inflammatory arthritis.
*   **Prevalence:** Affects 0.5% to 1% of the adult population.
*   **Gender Ratio:** 2–3:1 female-to-male ratio; estrogens likely play a role in pathogenesis.
*   **Peak Incidence:** Most commonly diagnosed between ages 25–55. Note: 33% of cases manifest after age 60.
*   **Genetic Markers:** **HLA-DR4** (DRB1*0401) is present in 70% of patients with defined RA. 
*   **Genetic Variant:** The **PTPN22** variant is specifically associated with Anti-CCP positive disease.

**Pathogenesis**
*   **Primary Pathogenic Mechanism:** Loss of self-tolerance. 
*   **Cellular Infiltrate:** Predominantly **CD4+ T lymphocytes**. **B lymphocytes** differentiate into plasma cells, producing Rheumatoid Factor (RF) and Anti-CCP.
*   **Seroconversion:** Anti-CCP and RF are detectable in serum years before clinical onset.

**Risk Factors**
*   **Smoking:** Strongly correlated with Anti-CCP and RF positive status. 
*   **Environmental:** Exposure to silica dust and mineral oils.
*   **Infectious Triggers:** Potential links to EBV, Mycoplasma, and Parvovirus B19.

## 2. RA Clinical Presentation and Joint Involvement

**Initial Symptoms**
*   **Undifferentiated Inflammatory Arthritis:** Category for early patients not yet meeting full classification criteria.
*   **Morning Stiffness:** Diagnostic Hallmark. Lasts **>1 hour** and characteristically improves with physical activity.

**Joint Distribution and Physical Exam**
RA presents as a symmetric polyarthritis, typically involving small joints.
*   **Target Joints:** MCP, PIP, MTP, and wrists (carpus).
*   **Sparing:** Distal Interphalangeal (DIP) joints and the thoracolumbar spine are rarely affected.
*   **Axial Involvement:** Limited to the cervical spine (atlantoaxial subluxation in <10%).
*   **Defining Physical Sign:** **Tenosynovitis of the flexor tendons**. This leads to decreased range of motion, reduced grip strength, and characteristic trigger fingers or contractures.

**Hand Deformities**
*   **Ulnar deviation:** Palmar subluxation of the proximal phalanx at the MCP joints.
*   **"Z" deformity of the thumb:** MCP subluxation with hyperextension of the first IP joint.
*   **Swan-neck deformity:** PIP hyperextension with compensatory DIP flexion.
*   **Boutonnière deformity:** PIP flexion with DIP hyperextension.
*   **"Piano key" sign:** Subluxation/hypermobility of the distal ulna.

## 3. Extra-Articular Manifestations of RA

Extra-articular disease occurs in up to 40% of patients, correlating with high RF/Anti-CCP titers and smoking.

**Systemic Involvement**
*   **Cutaneous:** **Rheumatoid nodules** (30–40%) are firm and associated with RF+ and erosive disease. **Vasculitis** (<1%) presents as petechiae, purpura, or large leg ulcers.
*   **Pulmonary:** **Pleuritis** is the most common manifestation. Interstitial Lung Disease (ILD) with NSIP pattern indicates a poor prognosis. **Caplan Syndrome** is the association of RA pulmonary nodulosis + silicosis.
*   **Cardiac:** 
    *   **Main Cause of Death:** Ischemic Heart Disease (accelerated atherosclerosis).
    *   **Most Frequent Involvement:** Pericarditis (usually asymptomatic).
    *   **Most Common Valvulopathy:** **Mitral Reflux**.
*   **Ocular:** Episcleritis, scleritis, and Secondary Sjogren’s Syndrome (10%).

**Hematological Syndromes**
*   **Felty’s Syndrome:** Triad of **Neutropenia, Splenomegaly, and Nodular RA**.
*   **Malignancy:** High risk for B-cell Lymphoma.

## 4. RA Diagnosis and Classification Criteria

**Clinical Requirement (The Entry Rule)**
The ACR/EULAR criteria must *only* be applied to patients with **at least one joint with clinical synovitis** (swelling) not better explained by another disease (e.g., SLE, Gout, OA).

**ACR/EULAR 2010 Scoring Table (Score ≥6 = RA)**

| Category | Criteria | Points |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Joint Involvement** | 1 large joint | 0 |
| | 2–10 large joints | 1 |
| | 1–3 small joints | 2 |
| | 4–10 small joints | 3 |
| | >10 joints (at least 1 small) | 5 |
| **Serology** | Negative RF and Negative ACPA/Anti-CCP | 0 |
| | Low-positive RF or ACPA (≤3x ULN) | 2 |
| | High-positive RF or ACPA (>3x ULN) | 3 |
| **Acute-phase Reactants** | Normal CRP and ESR | 0 |
| | Abnormal CRP or ESR | 1 |
| **Duration of Symptoms** | <6 weeks | 0 |
| | ≥6 weeks | 1 |

**Imaging**
*   **X-Ray:** Shows periarticular osteopenia and marginal erosions.
*   **MRI:** Highly sensitive for **Bone Marrow Edema**, an early precursor to radiographic erosions.

## 5. RA Therapeutic Management and Algorithms

**Pharmacotherapy Tiers**
1.  **Symptomatic (NSAIDs):** Analgesic only; **does not modify disease progression**.
2.  **Glucocorticoids (GC):** "Bridging therapy." *Prophylaxis Rule:* Use bisphosphonates if Prednisone ≥5mg/day for >3 months.
3.  **Synthetic DMARDs (sDMARDs):** **Methotrexate (MTX) is the gold standard**. 
4.  **Biological DMARDs (bDMARDs):** Anti-TNF (Infliximab, Adalimumab, Etanercept) or Non-TNF (Rituximab, Tocilizumab).
5.  **JAK Inhibitors:** Oral agents (Tofacitinib). Monitor liver enzymes, lipids, and creatinine.

**Treat-to-Target Algorithm**
*   **Step 1:** Initiate **MTX Monotherapy** immediately upon diagnosis.
*   **Step 2 (3-Month Evaluation):** If activity remains moderate/high, escalate to **Triple Therapy** (MTX + Sulfasalazine + Hydroxychloroquine) or add a **Biologic (Anti-TNF)**.
*   **Step 3 (Refractory):** If Anti-TNF fails after 3 months, switch to a different Anti-TNF or a non-TNF biologic (e.g., Tocilizumab or Rituximab).

## 6. Systemic Sclerosis (SSc): Classification and Clinical Pillars

**Disease Subtypes**

| Feature | Limited SSc (lcSSc) | Diffuse SSc (dcSSc) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Skin Involvement** | Distal to elbows/knees, face only | Proximal limbs, trunk, and face |
| **Dermatological Signs** | **Facie murina** (inexpressive face) | **Salt and pepper** pigmentation |
| **Raynaud's** | Precedes skin lesions by years | Concurrent with skin lesions |
| **Physical Exam** | Slow progression | **Tendon friction rubs** (high risk) |
| **Major Organ Risks** | Isolated PH (10-15%) | ILD, Scleroderma Renal Crisis |

**Raynaud’s Phenomenon**
*   **Primary:** Onset <30 years, normal capillaroscopy, negative ANA.
*   **Secondary:** Onset >30 years, distorted capillary loops, positive ANA. Risk of digital gangrene.

## 7. Multi-Organ Involvement in SSc

**Gastrointestinal (90%)**
*   **Watermelon Stomach (GAVE):** Vasculopathy causing **recurrent digestive bleeding**, leading to chronic iron-deficiency anemia.
*   **Dysmotility:** GERD, esophageal fibrosis, and intestinal pseudo-obstruction.

**Pulmonary**
*   **ILD:** Leading cause of death (NSIP pattern).
*   **Pulmonary Hypertension (PH):** Screening requirement: **Annual Echocardiogram** for all patients.

**Scleroderma Renal Crisis (SRC) - MEDICAL EMERGENCY**
*   **Clinical Presentation:** Abrupt malignant hypertension and rapid renal failure.
*   **Anticipatory/Predictor Factors:** Palpable tendon friction rubs, pericardial effusion, and Anti-RNA Polymerase III.
*   **Pharmacological Trigger:** **Prednisone doses >15mg/day** are strongly associated with triggering SRC.
*   **Management:** Immediate **short-acting ACE inhibitors** (Captopril). Do *not* use ACEi as prophylaxis in normotensive patients.

## 8. Serological Profiling and Prognosis

**SSc Specific Antibodies**
| Antibody | Clinical Correlation |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Anti-Scl-70 (Topoisomerase I)** | Diffuse SSc; high risk of ILD. |
| **Anti-Centromere** | Limited SSc; Pulmonary Hypertension risk. |
| **Anti-RNA Polymerase III** | Diffuse SSc; **Renal Crisis**, malignancy risk. |
| **Anti-U1-RNP / Anti-U3-RNP** | High risk for **Pulmonary Hypertension**. |

**Overlap Serology Table**
| Antibody | Overlap Syndrome |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Anti-U1-RNP** | Mixed Connective Tissue Disease (MCTD). |
| **Anti-PM/Scl** | SSc / Polymyositis Overlap. |
| **Anti-Jo-1** | Antisynthetase Syndrome. |
